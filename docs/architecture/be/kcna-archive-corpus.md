# KCNA corpus storage

The database stores normalized source documents separately from immutable retrieval snapshots. `kcna_documents` is the searchable logical record. `kcna_retrievals` preserves every distinct raw response, its digest, retrieval time, transport metadata, and the manifest entry that led to it. `kcna_document_retrievals` links them many-to-many, so a historical page containing several articles stores its raw bytes once. The full Open Korean Historical Corpus record remains in `document_source_metadata`; its redacted `text` field is provenance only, while searchable content comes from the fetched raw page.

Each document is identified by a PostgreSQL 18 UUIDv7 minted with `kcna_uuidv7_at(document_published_at)`. PostgreSQL computes a shift from the current clock, calls its native `uuidv7(interval)`, and rejects IDs whose extracted timestamp differs from `document_published_at` by 25 milliseconds or more. Retrieval IDs use the same function with the actual retrieval timestamp. Natural unique constraints make repeated imports idempotent without deriving IDs in application code.

KCNA usually publishes a calendar date without a time of day. For those records, `document_published_at` is noon in `Asia/Pyongyang` on the published date, and `document_publication_precision` is `day`. Noon keeps UTC conversion on the same local date and gives stable chronological grouping without presenting an invented exact time. Dates inferred from a URL or article dateline use `inferred_day`. Exact source timestamps use `second` or `minute` and are not rounded to noon.

Languages use ISO 639-3 codes: `kor`, `eng`, `zho`, `rus`, `spa`, and `jpn`. A current `kcna.kp` article identifier shared across language routes names a row in `kcna_translation_groups`, which the documents of every edition reference; the row records how the link was made (`source_identifier` today, `inferred` reserved for content-based matching). Historical pages have no group until a defensible correspondence is established: pairing English and Spanish pages by file number was tested and is wrong. Categories are rows in `kcna_categories`, scoped by source and language, referenced by a foreign key instead of repeated as free text. The source URL, element index, source record identifier, original date string, category, copyright label, complete metadata JSON, normalized title and body, raw bytes, charset, media type, hashes, and retrieval history remain available independently. Secondary records that contain only a headline retain a null body instead of a redaction placeholder.

The raw filesystem remains the recovery copy and source for re-extraction. PostgreSQL is the research index and query store, not the only archive. Importers never discard an older raw body when KCNA changes a page; a new digest creates another retrieval row while the logical document records its first and last retrieval times. Content digests and canonical URLs have separate B-tree indexes for duplicate and provenance lookups, and publication date indexes serve browsing.

Full-text search does not run in PostgreSQL. The search index described in [archive serving](kcna-archive-serving.md) is derived from these tables and can be rebuilt from them at any time with `database snapshot --rebuild`.

## Copies

The live site, the historical mirror, and the public dataset overlap: since December 2023 several thousand articles exist in two or three sources. After every import `importer/copies.rs` groups documents by language, publication date, and normalized title, splits each group by a digest of the normalized text so repeated headlines with different bodies stay apart, and points every duplicate at one preferred copy through `document_canonical_document_id`, a nullable self-reference. The preferred copy is chosen by source: `kcna.kp`, then `kcna.co.jp`, then the public dataset. A headline-only record joins an article only when its title names exactly one. The pass writes only the links that changed. On 2026-11-21 it folded 9,682 copies into 6,306 articles. Search indexes the preferred copy and names the other sources; the copies stay stored and reachable from it.

## Extraction

Historical pages come in two shapes. Multi-article pages mark each article with a numbered anchor and heading; the text between consecutive markers is the body, and an empty heading is filled from the sibling daily index page. Single-article pages open with a bold navigation breadcrumb, so the title is the bold text of the left-aligned title block, or failing that the first bold text that is not navigation (linked, inside a link, or carrying `>>`), or the first left-aligned text. Their body is the largest content cell when the layout has one and the whole page otherwise, in which case the navigation breadcrumb and repeated title before the article and the copyright footer after it are removed. Bodies keep paragraph structure: block elements and line breaks end a paragraph, paragraphs are whitespace-normalized, and they are joined by blank lines. A record whose page has no extractable title or body is still stored, with a placeholder title or null body and a `_kcna_archive` note in its metadata, so the public inventory stays fully represented. Current `kcna.kp` pages are semantic (`article h1`, `article p`) and must have both a title and a body; their category is the name in the list page title, with the opaque category identifiers kept in the metadata.

The public inventory ends in June 2025, while the mirror published until October 2025. After the listed records, the importer walks the stored `calendar` and `item` trees and imports every article-shaped page the inventory does not name. The file name gives the date and language (`calendar/2025/06/06-20/2025-0620-001.html` is Korean; an `ee` or `ss` suffix under `item` is English or Spanish), the precision is `inferred_day`, there is no source record identifier, and the metadata notes that the record is absent from the inventory. If a later inventory lists such a page, its record replaces the inferred one under the same natural key.

Parsing and hashing are CPU-bound and run on the blocking pool, one page per task, bounded by the core count. Each batch of records is stored in one transaction.

## Re-imports

Imports are idempotent on the natural key (source, canonical URL, element index, language). Each document stores a fingerprint of everything the importer would write, including a format version that is raised when extraction changes. A document whose fingerprint equals the stored one is skipped, so a routine re-import writes only what is new or changed: an unchanged import of all 409,584 documents takes 20 s and updates no rows, against 1 m 57 s for a full rewrite. A document identifier encodes its publication time, so a corrected date cannot be an in-place update: rows whose stored publication date, precision, or exact time differ from the incoming record are deleted in the same transaction and inserted again under a new identifier. Retrieval rows are keyed by requested URL and raw digest and are never rewritten.
