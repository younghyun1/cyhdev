# Minecraft map

The public Minecraft page uses squaremap terrain with a native Leaflet client. Its scoped palette, beveled controls, square outlines, monospace labels, and pixelated terrain evoke Minecraft without importing another component framework. Terrain fills the space between the website bars on desktop and mobile. The dimension selector, refresh action, and compact tool buttons float on the map. Tools open by click and close through their toggle, close button, or Escape; no sidebar or atlas header remains. Native inputs, visible keyboard focus, coordinate navigation, and text result lists complement pointer interaction.

The Layers menu surveys a selected area, shows biome colors or surface elevation, filters sampled biomes, and lists saved structure starts. Select area enables pointer dragging over complete chunks, capped at 128 by 128 blocks; otherwise a survey uses that size around the selected point. A Y selector samples cave biomes while elevation continues to describe the surface. Gold outlines identify completed surveys; the next survey remains separately outlined. Coverage counts, time, and incomplete results stay visible. Structure bounds describe saved metadata and can outlast a demolished structure.

Predicted biomes are enabled by default for the Overworld, requesting a bounded 128 by 128 block area near the map center after movement settles. Layers provides the toggle and fixed Y selector, initially Y 64. Muted cells appear only where the response confirms ungenerated chunks; actual survey cells and block matches suppress predictions throughout their chunks. A one-pixel dark outline with subtle two-pixel light casing marks the perimeter of predicted coverage, including holes, without drawing internal chunk boundaries. The outermost predicted cells use lower opacity; no prediction color extends into actual or uncertain coverage. Generated, unknown, and excluded coverage never admits predictions. The compact receipt labels the result approximate and shows the server-selected default or large-biomes preset. Coverage expires after at most 15 seconds, is renewed while visible, and clears on terrain refresh, dimension change, page hiding, or disabling prediction. Automatic work uses a single pending background slot below explicit actions and hover queries. The browser receives no seed and cannot override the preset. Predictions do not replace actual surface elevation or block-search results.

Hovering terrain shows coordinates and biome details independently of overlay visibility. Tapping or navigating to coordinates exposes the same readout. Existing survey samples take precedence over permitted, unexpired seed predictions. The panel labels observed versus predicted biomes, the four-block sample resolution, fixed biome sampling Y when applicable, and surface elevation at the sample origin when observed. Exact block names and Y coordinates come only from block-search matches at that X/Z; squaremap pixels cannot identify arbitrary blocks. The panel leaves pointer gestures unobstructed and does not announce continuous pointer updates through a live region.

When no reusable biome sample exists, a stationary hover requests one chunk after 500 milliseconds. The browser retains at most 64 chunk results for 30 seconds, including unavailable results, and shares a single request slot and one-second completion cooldown with explicit actions and background prediction. Explicit actions have priority over pending hover work. Dimension changes, terrain refresh, and hiding invalidate hover data; pointer movement never cancels an in-flight server read or queues a trail of requests. Missing terrain and request failures remain explicit.

The Blocks menu searches a selected area of at most 64 by 64 blocks and an inclusive vertical range of at most 512 layers. A larger dragged selection produces a size error; without a custom selection the search uses 64 by 64 blocks around the selected point. Cyan markers and a result list identify exact matches; results stop at 512 and report truncation. Dimension changes discard old observations and ignore late responses. Surveys and block searches follow explicit actions. Terrain and published player snapshots refresh separately while visible; player labels show persistent names and configured head, health, and armor details. Refresh requests made while tiles load are coalesced and queued, with completion and missing-tile feedback. Base terrain does not wait for the world-analysis catalog.

Places are public shared waypoints, loaded automatically when world metadata becomes available even while all menus are closed. Administrator controls create, edit, and delete them; the backend independently checks current authority. A location link preserves the selected dimension and coordinates. Travel tools provide coordinate navigation, spawn navigation, a horizontal distance ruler, and Overworld/Nether coordinate conversion. The conversion estimates a portal location, not an established link.

The original squaremap document is retired. Its former entry URLs redirect to `/minecraft`; only canonical JSON and PNG data remain served. The native explorer uses text nodes for all Leaflet labels. Plugin catalog failure leaves terrain and coordinate tools available; waypoint editing and analysis remain unavailable until their world metadata loads.
